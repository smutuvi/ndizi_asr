Core Thesis for Publication
Apparent high Word Error Rates (35.9%+) on spontaneous East African field interviews stem from three compounding factors: (1) unnormalized glued punctuation, MacRoman/UTF-8 mojibake (‚Ä¶, √â), Swahili compound boundary drift, and Bantu L1 phonological variation (~8–12 pp artificial penalty); (2) silent truncation of clips exceeding Whisper's 30s positional window (27.7% of the dataset); and (3) acoustic domain mismatch when freezing the encoder vs. catastrophic 51-language forgetting when unfreezing it. Addressing all three with 100%-frozen-base encoder+decoder rsLoRA, MMS_FA forced-aligned training segmentation, and overlap-VAD + LCS boundary stitching cuts single-model Fair WER to 15.30% (14.98% with EmbeddingGemma MBR) while retaining 100% of the 51-language base weights.

Methodology & Technical Contributions

1. 51-Language-Preserving Encoder+Decoder rsLoRA Training Architecture

Unfreezing Whisper encoder layers directly risks catastrophic forgetting across SunflowerASR-51's 50 other African languages. We freeze 100% of the base backbone (freeze_encoder: true, unfreeze_top_encoder_layers: 0) and attach rank-stabilized LoRA (use_rslora: true, r=32, alpha=64, dropout 0.05) across all attention and MLP projections (q_proj, k_proj, v_proj, out_proj, fc1, fc2).

Differential learning rates (1.5e-5 on encoder LoRA adapters, 4.0e-5 on decoder LoRA adapters) paired with SpecAugment (mask_time_prob=0.05, mask_feature_prob=0.04) adapt the acoustic front-end to rural field acoustics while preserving the exact base checkpoint.

2. MMS_FA Forced Alignment + Overlap-VAD LCS Stitching Long-Audio Pipeline

Training time: 1,743 train clips and 391 validation clips exceed 30s. Rather than dropping them, long_audio_chunker.py runs GPU-accelerated torchaudio.pipelines.MMS_FA word-level forced alignment to split long recordings at natural word pauses into ≤28.0s segments (expanding train from 5,572 to 7,610 segments with 0 VAD fallbacks).

Inference time: Long test utterances are segmented via Silero-VAD with a 1.5s sliding overlap window (overlap_s=1.5) and merged using normalized token-level Longest Common Subsequence (_stitch_chunk_texts_lcs) to eliminate boundary word truncation and duplication.

Category Phenomenon in Field Transcripts Representative Examples Normalizer Rule
1. Encoding & Punctuation Glued punctuation & MacRoman/UTF-8 mojibake from spreadsheet exports habari,mzuri | ‚Ä¶ | ‚ä¶ | √â | â€™ Case-insensitive regex replaces mojibake and punctuation with spaces prior to tokenization (in both Fixed and Fair scorers).
2. Oral Backchannels Inconsistent annotator inclusion of interviewer/farmer hesitations eeh, eeee, mmmh, mhm, aah, ooh, uuh Stripped before character-repeat collapsing so elongated backchannels (Eeee) do not collapse into single-letter tokens (e).
3. Numerals vs. Words Annotators mix Arabic numerals with spoken Swahili number words 2 → mbili, 15 → kumi na tano Deterministic cardinal number parser converts digits 0–999,999 into canonical spoken Swahili words.
4. Compound Boundaries Locative -ni, habitual hu-, and reduplicated adverbs written open or closed barabara ni → barabarani | mara kwa mara → marakwamara Bidirectional compound canonicalizer merges split prefixes/locatives and standardizes frequent multi-word agricultural expressions.
5. Bantu L1 Phonology Alveolar liquid neutralization (R/L) and initial glottal H-dropping tayari ↔ tayali | kula ↔ kura | hela ↔ ela Maps curated agricultural/everyday R/L and H-drop lexical doublets to a single canonical lemma without altering minimal pairs.
